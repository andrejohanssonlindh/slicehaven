# Director's notes — 2026-10-25

*The director's notes from mobile, after session 1. These are **hopes and ambitions, not hard requirements**: a foundation to build on and expand. The original text is kept word for word at the bottom; my structured English reading comes first.*

## Answers to session 1 questions
- **Names:** keep Eon's names (Mundana, Asharien…).
- **Party size:** variable, **max 4**. Early game 1 hero, later usually 2. Some heroes can sometimes use **3 dice** in a turn, which burns through your dice fast.
- **Language:** game text in English.

---

## Movement
Movement types: **walk, flight, jump, teleport…**
- **Walk:** any path. Tap the hexes you want to pass through, then confirm. Either way, show reachable hexes lit up when you choose move (like Civ).
- **Jump:** straight lines only, but passes over obstacles.
- Plot path → confirm is probably the same for every type.
- Odd rules are possible: e.g. teleport only 5–9 hexes away, walk only an odd number of steps.
- Gear/potions can add steps or turn movement into another type.
- Early game: almost everything is walk, some jump.

## Party traits (reputation-like)
- Set by **what you do**. Generous choices in events raise generosity, aggressive choices raise aggression, etc. Higher levels unlock options later.
- A choice can give + in one trait and − in another. Maybe **4 dimensions, each with +/−**.
- **Acting against your trait** past a certain level needs a die roll. Example: aggressive level 2 but you want to be kind → roll a d6, 4 out of 6 faces let you.
- Acting in line with your trait: no roll.
- Staying **neutral** lets you choose freely each time, but you get none of the trait bonuses.

## Dice in general
- A die usually has similar effects (e.g. mostly attack), with exceptions.
- Some heroes may have very mixed dice.
- **Low-end dice could be more mixed**, so harder to control. One way to make "bad" dice besides just lower values.
- **New dice on level-up**: a choice of a couple, pick one.
- Plan for **respec**: swap dice.
- Possible limit: you can carry X dice; only 1 of the highest tier, 2 of the next tier, and so on.
- **Heroes have different numbers of dice. Dice have different numbers of sides.**

## Dice effects
- Base: **damage, shield, heal**.
- **Bleed:** damage over time.
- **Rend armour:** more damage against armoured enemies. Better than poison against armour, but not useless otherwise.
- **Poison:** increases damage taken in general.
- **Disease:** weakens the target (maybe physical actions).
- Dice should have **classes/types** that can be buffed and nerfed in different ways.
- Debuffs like **disadvantage**: fewer dice can be played.
- Monsters can be **weak or resistant** to different damage types.

## Heroes & progression
- Heroes level up and can **become something else**, gaining new dice.
- Some heroes are **multi-path** (can become different things); some are only one thing. Tied to level. E.g. the shepherd is only a shepherd at level 1, then changes into one of X options.
- Like Gloomhaven, heroes have **personal quests** that "complete" them. But a completed hero shouldn't be gone for good. Rather, not always available, but you can sometimes bring them along. Maybe a **cooldown** (shared between heroes).
- Most, if not all, of the **better heroes with long level paths come from chained quests**.
- Consider Gloomhaven-style **difficulty**: average party level sets the scenario level, and enemies scale with it.

## Story
- **Start with one character: a shepherd.**
- **Starting hook:** you live alone on your farm with your mother and older sister. Your father died in "the war" (tie to an Eon war). Poor family; bad harvest this year, not enough money for food. You need to take odd jobs.
- **Early missions are small and local, in the village:** rats in the merchant's cellar, wolves outside someone's farm…
- **Each early mission adds a character until you're 4:** the smith's son (tank?), the herbalist's apprentice, the hunter's daughter who isn't allowed into the forest with her brothers… At least one of these should be worth playing for a long time.
- **Hard decisions:** e.g. a mission that requires permanently sacrificing one of your companions.
- **Hook:** after some mission, a boss (maybe a human? a mad elf?) carries a **letter that talks about the main character's father as if he were alive**.
- **Start very basic.** No magic, or at most a herbalist / a simple bard without magic. Later in the campaign you run into new kinds of magic and concepts, which slowly unlock new characters.
