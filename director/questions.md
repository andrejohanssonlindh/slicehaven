### Q30 — People's bodies: did I read you right? *(open; holds up A2)*
My reading of "a super simple polygon with shells over": each person becomes a small 3D body (boxes or a few faces for the torso, head and limbs), seen from any side, walking with moving limbs. The first shell gives the clothes their colour, and the second sits a little outside it and breaks the edges into pixels, most of all on the limbs. That's the same trick the brief already uses on the world. Plan: one person built this way, standing next to today's flat card, for you to judge on the phone before anything else changes. Right?

### Q29 — The live world and rounds *(open; holds up A3, and through it combat, sneaking and magic; not needed for the next step)*
The world runs in real time; the ruleset's conflicts run in rounds. How do they meet? My lean: **A) like Fallout 1–2**: you walk in real time, and a fight (or a tense moment: sneaking past a guard, a social conflict) switches to rounds, with the world held still between turns. Other options: **B)** always real time, with rounds only as a hidden clock (a round = so many seconds); **C)** real time with pause, where you give orders while it's paused.

### Q28 — Turning the view: how far? *(open; holds up A1. I'll go with my lean unless you say otherwise)*
Two arrows swing the camera round the hero. **A) four views, a quarter turn each** (my lean: the pixel look stays crisp, and rooms, cut-aways and art only need to be right from four sides); **B) eight views, an eighth of a turn each** (smoother, but diagonal views shimmer more in the pixel look and the walls are drawn at an angle); **C) free turning** by dragging. A can grow into B later.
